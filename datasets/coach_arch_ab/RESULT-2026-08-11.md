# Result: coach architecture A/B, 2026-08-11

**Intended setup: both arms on Claude `claude-sonnet-4-6`, the same judge, the same 21 cases.** The judge is the harness default free model, `openrouter` / `nvidia/nemotron-3-super-120b-a12b:free`. Twenty cases counted. `cx-adv-003` errored on arm B and is excluded from **both** arms rather than scored as a failure, because an errored case is not evidence about an agent.

Runs: `20260811T104144-coach_v2_arch-claude` (arm A), `20260811T153415-coach_v3_arch-claude` (arm B).

## Validity: the actionability result is withdrawn

The `answer_actionability` delta reported below is **withdrawn**. It is not a result. Two problems, both mine.

**1. The judge is unstable on this criterion.** Five of the failing cases were re-judged on an **unchanged** configuration. The judge passed 3 of those 5 on actionability where the original run passed 0 of 5. A criterion that moves that far with nothing changed cannot resolve a 10-point difference between two arms. That same follow-up also threw a hard `JUDGE_ERROR` on 1 of 10 judgments, so the criterion is not only noisy, it is intermittently unavailable.

**2. The arms may not have run the same model.** `COACH_FALLBACK_PROVIDERS=openrouter,anthropic` was set in the harness environment during the run. The coach builds its specialists through `withRoleFallback`, so any failed Anthropic call in arm B would have been answered silently by a free fallback model, with nothing in the output to say so. Arm A, the reconstructed single-call shape, had no fallback path at all. "Same model on both arms" was the intent of the setup, not a fact this run verified.

**What survives.** The 45-point completeness gap is far outside the noise band those two problems imply, and it stands. So does Finding 1, that the old coach README named the wrong failure mode. The 5-point safety difference was never presented as a finding, and it is not one now.

The actionability and combined rows are what the judge emitted. They are printed here because deleting an observation is its own kind of dishonesty, not because they support a conclusion. Read them as a record of what came back, and read nothing into the direction of the sign.

**The rebuild is supported on completeness.** "Materially better" can retire.

## Finding 1: the stated diagnosis was backwards

The coach README claimed the v2 design's weakness was cross-domain questions. Split by question type:

| Completeness | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-domain (5 cases) | 60.0% (3/5) | 80.0% (4/5) |
| **Single-domain (15 cases)** | **46.7% (7/15)** | **100% (15/15)** |

The v2 shape was shallow on **everything**, and slightly worse on simple questions than complex ones. The rebuild fixed single-domain completely and multi-domain only partially. Nothing in the code could have revealed this; code does not tell you what it is bad at.

## Finding 2, withdrawn: the actionability delta

The original write-up read the 10-point drop as a real cost of the rebuild and treated it as the counterweight criterion doing its job. That reading is retracted. Ten of twenty arm B answers were scored as failing to land on a number, a range, a frequency, or a decision rule, but the judge that produced that count passed 3 of 5 of those same answers when asked again with nothing changed. The observation cannot carry a 10-point claim, in either direction.

The design argument behind the criterion is still sound: an architecture can score well on completeness by touching every domain and committing to nothing in any of them, and a hedge is not a better answer. A counterweight criterion is worth having. This particular measurement of it is not, and the criterion cannot earn its place until the judge running it is stable enough to be re-measured.

Chasing the withdrawn number did surface a real retrieval defect in the coach, unrelated to how the judge scored anything: retrieval deduped by page-level `source` label instead of chunk id, so only the top-ranked chunk per page reached the model and its siblings were dropped. Fixed on the coach branch `fix/retrieval-dedupe-drops-sibling-chunks`. The open write-up lives at `centenarian-coach-multiagent/plans/11-actionability-regression.md`.

## Not scores, reported anyway

Citations are a **structural fact, not a score**: arm A has no retrieval and was never trying to cite. Publishing "0% versus 95%" there would be dishonest arithmetic. Latency belongs in the same sentence as the win, because arm B does roughly five times the work per answer.

## What would make this stronger

One run of 21 cases on three criteria is a small sample. A difference of one or two cases is not a finding: +45.0 clears that bar, +5.0 on safety does not, and the actionability delta fails for the separate and worse reason given under Validity.

Two fixes are required before this pack produces another actionability number:

1. **Stabilize the judge on `answer_actionability`.** Either move that criterion to a paid judge, or run it several times per case and publish the agreement rate alongside the score, or both. Judge idiosyncrasy on this project has already been measured at 47 points on one criterion, so a second judge is a check on architecture effects generally, not a substitute for fixing this one.
2. **Pin the agent model per arm and record what actually answered each call.** Unset `COACH_FALLBACK_PROVIDERS` for A/B runs, or log the resolved provider and model per specialist call so a silent fallback shows up in the result instead of hiding in it.
