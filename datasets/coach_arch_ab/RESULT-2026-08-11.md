## Finding 1: the stated diagnosis was backwards

The coach README claimed the v2 design's weakness was cross-domain questions. Split by question type:

| Completeness | Arm A | Arm B |
|---|---|---|
| Multi-domain (5 cases) | 60.0% (3/5) | 80.0% (4/5) |
| **Single-domain (15 cases)** | **46.7% (7/15)** | **100% (15/15)** |

The v2 shape was shallow on **everything**, and slightly worse on simple questions than complex ones. The rebuild fixed single-domain completely and multi-domain only partially. Nothing in the code could have revealed this; code does not tell you what it is bad at.

## Finding 2: the rebuild cost actionability

Ten of twenty current-build answers failed to land on a number, a range, a frequency, or a decision rule. Filed open at `centenarian-coach-multiagent/plans/11-actionability-regression.md`.

This is the counterweight criterion earning its place. Completeness alone would have shown a clean win. An architecture can score well on completeness by touching every domain and committing to nothing in any of them, and a hedge is not a better answer.

## Not scores, reported anyway

| Median latency | 9.2s | 45.2s |

Citations are a **structural fact, not a score**: arm A has no retrieval and was never trying to cite. Publishing "0% versus 95%" there would be dishonest arithmetic. Latency belongs in the same sentence as the win, because arm B does roughly five times the work per answer.

## What would make this stronger

One run of 21 cases on three criteria is a small sample. A difference of one or two cases is not a finding; the +45.0 and the −10.0 are, the +5.0 on safety is not. A second run on a different judge would separate architecture effects from judge idiosyncrasy, which this project has already measured at 47 points on one criterion.
