Transcript, Hitsville U.S.A., West Grand Boulevard, Detroit. Tour of the Motown Museum with docent Keisha. Berry Gordy Jr. founded Tamla Records in 1959 with an $800 loan from his family's Ber-Berry Co-op savings fund, incorporating Motown Record Corporation in 1960 — the name blends "motor" and "town." The house's Studio A stayed open around the clock; the docent calls it "the snake pit." Acts recorded here include Smokey Robinson and the Miracles, the Supremes, the Temptations, Stevie Wonder, Marvin Gaye and the Jackson 5. The museum panel says Motown released 110 top-ten Billboard pop hits between 1961 and 1971. Gordy ran quality control meetings every Friday morning where staff voted whether a record could compete with the best on the charts. The company moved to Los Angeles in 1972; Gordy's sister Esther Gordy Edwards preserved the house and opened the museum in 1985. The echo chamber cut into the ceiling is still functional, and visitors sing "My Girl" in Studio A at the tour's end.
