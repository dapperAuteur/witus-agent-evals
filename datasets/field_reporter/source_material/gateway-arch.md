Transcript, St. Louis riverfront. Ranger talk at the Gateway Arch visitor center. The ranger explains the Arch was designed by architect Eero Saarinen, who won a national design competition in 1948; construction ran 1963 to 1965. The interpretive panel calls it the tallest arch in the world and the tallest human-made monument in the Western Hemisphere at 630 feet — and the ranger points out the neat symmetry that it is also 630 feet wide at the base. It is a weighted catenary curve, clad in stainless steel. The tram to the top, invented by Dick Bowser, uses barrel-shaped capsules that rotate like Ferris-wheel cars. The Arch anchors Gateway Arch National Park, redesignated from the Jefferson National Expansion Memorial in 2018, commemorating St. Louis's role as the gateway to westward expansion after the Louisiana Purchase. The Old Courthouse nearby heard the Dred Scott case in 1847. On windy days the top can sway up to 18 inches, the ranger says, though an inch or two is typical.
