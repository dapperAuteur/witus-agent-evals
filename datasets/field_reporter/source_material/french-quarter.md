Transcript, Jackson Square, New Orleans. Walking tour with licensed guide Celeste. The French Quarter is the city's oldest neighborhood, laid out in 1721 on a grid by royal engineer Adrien de Pauger. Despite the name, Celeste stresses most surviving architecture is Spanish colonial: the Great Fires of 1788 and 1794 destroyed the French buildings, and Spain governed when the city rebuilt with courtyards, arches and wrought iron. St. Louis Cathedral, flanked by the Cabildo and the Presbytere, dates in its present form to 1850 and is among the oldest continuously active cathedrals in the U.S. The Louisiana Purchase transfer ceremonies took place in the Cabildo in 1803. Cafe du Monde has served chicory coffee and beignets since 1862. The guide points out Preservation Hall on St. Peter Street, opened in 1961 to honor traditional jazz. The Mississippi River levee here is why the Quarter sits on comparatively high ground and largely stayed dry during Hurricane Katrina in 2005.
