Transcript, Sugarlands Visitor Center trailhead, Great Smoky Mountains National Park. Interview with interpretive ranger Tom. The park straddles the Tennessee–North Carolina line and, per the ranger, is consistently the most-visited national park in the United States, with over 12 million visits a year. The "smoke" is fog produced when vegetation exhales volatile organic compounds that scatter blue light. The exhibit board calls the Smokies a global salamander capital: about 30 species, including many lungless salamanders that breathe through their skin. Elevations run from under 900 feet to 6,643 feet at Clingmans Dome, stacking ecosystems like a drive from Georgia to Maine. The park was established in 1934, assembled from over 6,000 small land purchases, partly funded by a $5 million Rockefeller family gift. Unlike most national parks it charges no entrance fee, a condition tied to the deed transferring Newfound Gap Road. Synchronous fireflies draw lottery-ticket crowds each June near Elkmont.
