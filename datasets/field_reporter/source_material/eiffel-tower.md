Transcript, Champ de Mars, Paris. Standing under the Eiffel Tower with guide Antoine from the Paris Walks collective. He says the tower was built for the 1889 Exposition Universelle, the world's fair marking the centennial of the French Revolution, and was engineered by Gustave Eiffel's company — the detailed structural design came from engineers Maurice Koechlin and Emile Nouguier. According to the plaque at the north pillar, construction took two years, two months and five days, finishing in March 1889. Antoine notes the tower stands about 330 meters tall today including antennas, and that it was the world's tallest structure until New York's Chrysler Building passed it in 1930. The onsite exhibition panel says roughly 18,000 iron pieces and 2.5 million rivets hold it together, and that it sways a few centimeters in strong wind. Artists including Guy de Maupassant famously protested it as an eyesore when it went up; a petition ran in Le Temps newspaper in 1887. Painters repaint it about every seven years by hand.
