Transcript, Seligman, Arizona. Chat with the owner of a souvenir shop on the historic strip. Seligman calls itself the "Birthplace of Historic Route 66" — after Interstate 40 bypassed the town in 1978, local barber Angel Delgadillo organized the association that got Arizona to designate the old road a historic highway in 1987. The shop's placard says Route 66 was commissioned in 1926, ran 2,448 miles from Chicago to Santa Monica, and was fully decommissioned in 1985. Angel's family ran the Snow Cap Drive-In next door, opened by his brother Juan in 1953, famous for gag menus and "dead chicken." Steinbeck named 66 "the Mother Road" in The Grapes of Wrath. The owner says the town's look — vintage cars, neon, characters — helped inspire Radiator Springs in the Pixar film Cars, whose crew interviewed Angel. Freight trains on the BNSF line still rumble past every few minutes, a reminder the railroad built the town first, in the 1880s.
